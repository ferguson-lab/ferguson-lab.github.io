    HOW ORBITAL TRACKING WORKS

    1. 1 Orbit. The laser focus sweeps a circle of radius r around the particle at f_orb. The emitter shines brightest when the beam passes closest to it — watch the ring light up on one side.
    2. 2 Demodulate. Each revolution's photon stream is folded into its first Fourier pair (a₁,b₁). Phase φ gives the escape direction; modulation depth gives distance d = κ(m)·σ²/r. The ratio of the two z-planes yields height — immune to brightness changes.
    3. 3 Feedback. The orbit center steps toward the estimate every revolution, closing the loop. When blinking drops the signal below gate, the tracker coasts, then spiral-searches until relock. Try a KICK, raise D, or enable bleaching.
    Green trail = true Brownian path · magenta trail = tracked stage path · ring color = measured photons per bin · dashed vector = tracking error. Brightness variations (blink / flicker / bleach) confound intensity-based z — hence the ratiometric dual-plane scheme.
